SR3H · Oxford

practical intelligence, engineered.

SR3H designs and builds useful software, and researches the systems that help increasingly capable language models work reliably with people.

We are interested in what surrounds the model: how human intent becomes structured context; how useful state and memory persist; how permissions and privacy are respected; and how decisions, actions and outcomes remain understandable and verifiable.

From specialist products and human–AI interfaces to the engineering systems behind them, we build to make powerful technology more useful, dependable and human.

Beyond the model

Making capable models work in real systems.

A capable model is only one component of a dependable product. We explore the systems around it: how intent is captured, relevant context is selected, state persists, memory is scoped, tools are authorised, decisions remain traceable and outputs can be checked.

The aim is not to make the model seem more intelligent. It is to give existing capability enough structure to remain useful, coherent and accountable over longer periods of work.

  1. Human intent
  2. Context and permissions
  3. Model and tools
  4. Persistent state and evidence
  5. Human control

Research systems

Persistent context, with deliberate boundaries.

Conchup and Brane explore two sides of the same question: how intelligent systems retain the context they need without taking control away from people.

Persistence for the work

Conchup

Persistent engineering context for long-running human–AI development. Conchup connects requirements, decisions, design, implementation, tests and release evidence into project state that people and models can work from.

It is being designed to identify the effect of a change, revise the affected work, and keep a clear record of what changed and why.

Conchup access by invitation

Context controlled by the person

Brane

Brane explores how a person can provide relevant, persistent context without handing over their digital identity or private information.

Its starting point is simple: what does a system need to know to do this job well — and what does it not need to know?

Relevant memory. Deliberate boundaries.

How we work

Quietly capable.

  • Start with the real problem. The useful version of a product is usually less complicated than the first brief.
  • Take craft seriously. Design, engineering and language all need to agree.
  • Keep people in the picture. Automation should improve judgment, not hide it.
  • Make room for possibility. Some work earns its value today; some opens a door for tomorrow.
