Oxford

Boyle, Hooke & the pressure of a gas

A plaque beside University College on the High Street marks the site where Boyle and Hooke carried out their air-pump experiments.

Boyle’s law describes how a gas’s pressure rises as its volume falls, at a constant temperature.

Oxford, science & engineering

From Oxford Instruments to Grassroots

Martin and Audrey Wood founded Oxford Instruments in 1959, helping turn Oxford’s magnet research into MRI technology. They later established Oxford Innovation, whose workspace business manages Grassroots.

Another connection: Oxford Instruments’ Nova 600 X-ray source drawings specify Swagelok cooling connections.

The Oxford Instruments story ↗The cooling connections ↗

Watching reactions at Diamond

At Harwell, Diamond Light Source uses intense X-rays to examine materials as they change. Its gas-flow catalysis cell uses Swagelok connections before and after the sample, allowing reactions to be studied under a controlled gas flow.

Swagelok-based cells also support experiments that follow batteries as they charge and discharge.

Other Swagelok & Oxford connections

From cryogenics to hydrogen and aviation fuels.

Oxford ↔ TexasVelocys · Oxford and Houston

Velocys uses microchannel reactors to turn synthesis gas into liquid hydrocarbons, which can be upgraded into sustainable aviation fuel. Its operations connect Oxford and Houston.

Velocys ↗
Britain’s first liquid helium

Kurt Mendelssohn first liquefied helium in Britain at Oxford’s Old Clarendon Laboratory on 13 January 1933. A landmark in the low-temperature physics that later underpinned superconducting magnets.

Oxford’s low-temperature history ↗
Hydrogen storage at Culham

The HyDUS demonstrator explores storing hydrogen in a solid metal hydride. Its stainless-steel hydrogen lines use Swagelok fittings; the 2026 report describes the integrated Culham system as not yet fully commissioned.

HyDUS project report ↗
Oxford Flow · from jet engines to valves

Research into pressure control for jet-engine testing led to Oxford Flow’s valves and regulators. An Oxford engineering idea with applications across industrial fluid systems.

The Oxford Flow story ↗
OXCCU · fuel from carbon dioxide

Oxford spin-out OXCCU combines carbon dioxide and hydrogen to make jet-fuel-range hydrocarbons. Its OX1 demonstration plant is at Oxford Airport.

Oxford’s OXCCU story ↗
Oxford Thermofluids Institute · Osney

High-speed gas flow, turbine cooling and heat transfer are studied at Osney. The institute grew from research in a former power station, with a longstanding Rolls-Royce connection.

The institute’s history ↗
Immersion cooling at Pangbourne

Just beyond Oxfordshire, Castrol’s Pangbourne research explores liquid immersion cooling for computing. Servers are immersed in a specialist fluid to carry heat away.
