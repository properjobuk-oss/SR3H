Computing’s third great transformation

We may be living through the start of computing’s third great transformation.

→→01

Personal computing made computing power widely accessible. The internet connected people, information and businesses. Conversational and agentic AI is beginning to change how people interact with computers and what computers can do for them.

It is the combination of dialogue, reasoning support and action capability that matters here. The possible impact could rival earlier transformations, but its scale, timing and distribution remain uncertain.

1980s–1990s · Computing

Personal computing

People operate computers.

Explore this idea

Personal computers put general-purpose software on desks and in homes. People operated applications to create digital work. The internet and AI (Artificial intelligence) build on that foundation.

1990s–2000s · Connectivity

The internet

People navigate the digital world.

Explore this idea

Websites, search, email and online services connected people and businesses. Customers learned to find information and work across different interfaces.

2020s onwards · Intelligence

Conversational and agentic AI

People collaborate with computers.

Explore this idea

Generative AI creates content. Large language models support dialogue. Agents can use connected tools within defined permissions. A fluent conversation does not establish reliability or authority.

01.02

What the evidence cannot tell us

Rapid adoption and spending do not guarantee profitable business models, reliable systems or proportional productivity gains. Existing devices and internet infrastructure make trying an AI service easier than adopting an entirely new computing platform.

The historical eras overlap. A comparison across technologies depends on the population, survey question, geography and starting point. There is no universal adoption clock.
