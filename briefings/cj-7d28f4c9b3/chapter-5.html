The AI-enabled industrial distributor

PROPOSAL · No pilot agreed.

What could an AI-enabled industrial distributor look like?

→→05

How could AI (Artificial intelligence) make an already sophisticated industrial distributor easier to work with, without weakening specialist relationships? Start with the work people already do well and look for an incremental improvement.

These are potential applications for discussion. They do not describe a verified Swagelok Southeast Texas internal process or an agreed pilot.

Opportunity 01 · Proposed

Repeat orders and replenishment

Known products. Established customers. Recurring demand.

Explore this idea

Existing portals, ERP, EDI, email and phone may already serve repeat orders well. An assistant could prepare a request using approved history. Confirmation would require identity, current data and authority. No repeat-order percentage or saving is assumed.

Opportunity 02 · Proposed

Modified or more complex orders

Changes in specification. Clarification. Quotation requirements.

Explore this idea

Collect the request, clarify missing information and prepare a complete handoff. This may help with iterative or modified enquiries. A person checks the quote; no binding price, stock, suitability or order commitment is implied.

Opportunity 03 · Proposed

Engineered solutions and specialist support

Application knowledge. Design. Technical collaboration.

Explore this idea

Organise project context, drawings and questions for a specialist. Qualified people retain responsibility for design, compatibility and safety. Published services do not establish local system access or permission to reuse knowledge.

05.01

A service connected to people and systems

Customer’s assistant → authorised commercial service → Swagelok people and approved systems. Shared product knowledge might be reusable across a network; customer data, prices, inventory and commercial authority must remain appropriately local.

05.02

A practical starting point

Consider quotation assistance for one approved product category: structured intake, retrieval from a curated knowledge set, clarification of missing information and a complete handoff to a person. Start alongside existing systems, possibly with controlled uploaded data.

Unchanged repeat requests could be confirmable only within approved limits and with authority. Standard changes prepare a quote; unusual needs go to a specialist; discounts or larger commitments go to an account manager. No order is placed by this briefing.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE · LOCAL ONLY

Walk through a quotation request

01 / A request arrives

Fictional customer: “We need the same items as our approved list, with one change.” No real parts, prices or customer records are used.

02 / Clarify the change

Collect the list reference, requested change, quantity and delivery need. Flag technical questions for a qualified specialist; do not infer compatibility.

03 / Prepare the handoff

Organise the request and outstanding questions for a person to review. No quote is issued and no order is placed.

Proposed intake pattern. No live AI, supplier integration or transaction.

05.03

Measure before claiming an improvement

Potential benefits are better customer experience, greater efficiency, opportunity for growth and a practical starting point. Each is a hypothesis to measure.

Establish a baseline for request completeness, clarification rounds, handling time, customer effort, conversion, specialist capacity and errors. A shorter interaction is not an improvement if it loses important technical information.

05.04

Questions for our conversation

Where do requests become difficult, and what would this improve rather than duplicate? Which product information and interfaces could be used with permission? Where must technical judgement and commercial approval remain central? What could be reused across distributors, and what must stay local? Is there a narrow, measurable test with an agreed sponsor?

THE THOUGHT TO TAKE FORWARD

A useful first test would be narrow enough to measure, safe enough to run alongside today’s process, and valuable enough for a customer and the team to choose again.
