Computing’s third great transformation

We may be living through the start of computing’s third great transformation.

→→01

Personal computing made computing power widely accessible. The internet connected people, information and businesses. Conversational and agentic AI is beginning to change how people interact with computers and what computers can do for them.

It is the combination of dialogue, reasoning support and action capability that matters here. The possible impact could rival earlier transformations, but its scale, timing and distribution remain uncertain.

1980s–1990s · Computing

Personal computing

People operate computers.

Explore this idea

Personal computers put general-purpose software on desks and in homes. People operated applications to create digital work. The internet and AI (Artificial intelligence) build on that foundation.

1990s–2000s · Connectivity

The internet

People navigate the digital world.

Explore this idea

Websites, search, email and online services connected people and businesses. Customers learned to find information and work across different interfaces.

2020s onwards · Intelligence

Conversational and agentic AI

People collaborate with computers.

Explore this idea

Generative AI creates content. Large language models support dialogue. Agents can use connected tools within defined permissions. A fluent conversation does not establish reliability or authority.

01.02

What the evidence cannot tell us

Rapid adoption and spending do not guarantee profitable business models, reliable systems or proportional productivity gains. Existing devices and internet infrastructure make trying an AI service easier than adopting an entirely new computing platform.

The historical eras overlap. A comparison across technologies depends on the population, survey question, geography and starting point. There is no universal adoption clock.

The customer relationship is changing

The customer relationship is moving beyond the supplier’s website.

→→02

Customers can explore a problem, develop a decision and interact with businesses within a chosen AI (Artificial intelligence) environment, sometimes before a supplier knows that an opportunity exists.

Today, a customer may move among search, supplier websites, portals, email and phone. A connected assistant can bring parts of that work into a continuing conversation. Search and supplier websites will also keep evolving.

Situation 01 · Illustration

Thinking without a supplier

“Why is this room always cold?”

Explore this idea

A customer explores a heating problem, budget and preferences before considering a supplier. The conversation may shape later demand. This is a fictional example.

Situation 02 · Illustration

Identifying and comparing

“What are my options, and how do they differ?”

Explore this idea

An assistant can help identify suppliers and comparison criteria. Missing information, errors and commercial incentives still matter. Being mentioned is not the same as being recommended.

Situation 03 · Illustration

Working with a known supplier

“Help me prepare this request for our supplier.”

Explore this idea

The customer deliberately opens an authorised supplier service. It exposes only the information and actions actually supported. This is different from spontaneous recommendation; no particular Swagelok plugin is assumed.

02.02

Three reasons to stay

Continuity: retain useful context where the system supports it and the user permits it. Convenience: research, comparison and authorised actions may be available in one place. Intelligence: combine general reasoning with approved specialist knowledge and current business information.

Leaving the assistant does not necessarily mean losing intelligence: a supplier website may use equivalent models. The practical friction may be repeating accumulated context or interrupting the workflow.

Notes & sources N05 ↗
02.03

Chosen does not mean controlled

Memory and personalisation depend on settings, access and platform rules. Familiarity or feeling understood does not establish correctness, better decisions or durable loyalty. Paid access does not by itself establish freedom from advertising or commercial influence.

A study of ChatGPT consumer use found practical guidance, information seeking and writing to be prominent activities. That supports a broader view of use than search alone; it does not prove improved decisions or reveal any individual customer’s private conversations.

Three questions for every business

Three questions every business owner should be asking.

→→03

As customers increasingly think, make decisions and interact with businesses through AI, three questions become useful. They are overlapping business capabilities, not a compulsory sequence.

01 · Will AI discover us?

Discover

Will AI (Artificial intelligence) identify your business when you’re relevant to a customer’s needs?

Explore this idea

Make service areas and capabilities clear in authoritative, machine-readable information. This may help an assistant recognise a relevant business, but cannot guarantee inclusion or ranking.

02 · Will AI understand us?

Understand

Can AI accurately understand your products, capabilities and what makes you different?

Explore this idea

Publish current capabilities, distinctions and supporting evidence. An assistant should be able to tell what you do, where you do it and what makes you different. Being named is not enough.

03 · Can customers work with us?

Do Business

Can customers interact with your services without leaving their preferred AI environment?

Explore this idea

Approved tools can support enquiries, quotation requests or transactions where implemented. APIs define software interfaces; MCP (Model Context Protocol) connects tools and context; A2A (Agent2Agent) supports agent coordination. None grants commercial authority.

03.01

The competitive implication

Businesses that are easier for AI to discover, understand and work with may gain an advantage over equally capable competitors that remain difficult to access. This is a strategic proposition, not demonstrated revenue uplift or a universal instruction to build an agent immediately.

Notes & sources N07 ↗
03.02

What makes these capabilities possible?

Discover: clear, machine-readable information, evidence on visibility and a useful understanding of commercial questions. Understand: structured identity, authoritative sources, verified claims and a clear match between capabilities and customer requirements. Do Business: approved plugins and tools, integration, identity, authorisation and human approval.

A tool connection can retrieve information or invoke a service. It does not establish autonomous agency. A plugin available for deliberate use does not guarantee spontaneous recommendation.

From conversation to commercial action

AI (Artificial intelligence) is starting to do business, not just talk about it.

→→04

Connected assistants can bring information and specific business actions into a conversation. The important questions are what the service can actually do, for whom, and under whose authority.

The examples below distinguish an announcement, a documented checkout route and a development capability. None establishes that SR3H has built the corresponding service.

Market · Announced capability

Walmart

A retailer brings shopping closer to the conversation.

Explore this idea

Walmart’s October 2025 announcement described planned shopping through ChatGPT. It establishes intent, not today’s availability. Geography, account requirements, Sparky integration, checkout and fulfilment still need a current end-to-end check.

Notes & sources N09 ↗
Market · Documented route

Shopify

Discovery and checkout can be different steps.

Explore this idea

Shopify documents ChatGPT as a discovery channel, with checkout on the merchant’s store in an in-app browser or new web tab. Other supported channels may offer direct checkout. Merchant and country eligibility vary; no purchase was tested here.

Notes & sources N10 ↗
Platform · Development capability

ChatGPT plugins

Connect approved tools, information and services.

Explore this idea

OpenAI documents plugins that connect instructions, tools and services through MCP (Model Context Protocol). Actions depend on installation, authentication and permissions. This does not establish that a Swagelok service is published or authorised to place orders.

Notes & sources N08 ↗
04.01

Three horizons, viewed from October 2026

Today: bounded connected services, with capability and eligibility established separately for each market example. Next 12 months: richer controlled workflows, such as preparing a specialist quotation, are possibilities to examine through October 2027. Beyond: independently operated agents may coordinate authorised work. These are scenarios, not delivery commitments.

04.02

A possible industrial workflow

A maintenance system identifies a need. A purchasing agent receives an approved requirement, budget and delivery constraints. An approved supplier service consults authoritative product information and, only where connected and permitted, customer-specific pricing and stock.

The systems clarify permitted ambiguities and prepare a proposal. A person gives required approval. An accepted order is recorded in the authoritative systems. Exceptions, technical suitability and safety go to qualified people.

Notes & sources N11 ↗
04.03

Connections are not commercial authority

Model Context Protocol (MCP) supports connections to tools and context. Agent2Agent (A2A (Agent2Agent)) supports coordination among independently operated agents, including tasks, status and results. They can complement one another; two organisations exchanging information do not automatically need A2A.

Identity, permissions, reliable data, policy enforcement, approval thresholds, audit records and commercial responsibility remain necessary. Existing procurement platforms, electronic data interchange and sophisticated chatbots already support parts of these processes.

The AI-enabled industrial distributor

PROPOSAL · No pilot agreed.

What could an AI-enabled industrial distributor look like?

→→05

How could AI (Artificial intelligence) make an already sophisticated industrial distributor easier to work with, without weakening specialist relationships? Start with the work people already do well and look for an incremental improvement.

These are potential applications for discussion. They do not describe a verified Swagelok Southeast Texas internal process or an agreed pilot.

Opportunity 01 · Proposed

Repeat orders and replenishment

Known products. Established customers. Recurring demand.

Explore this idea

Existing portals, ERP, EDI, email and phone may already serve repeat orders well. An assistant could prepare a request using approved history. Confirmation would require identity, current data and authority. No repeat-order percentage or saving is assumed.

Opportunity 02 · Proposed

Modified or more complex orders

Changes in specification. Clarification. Quotation requirements.

Explore this idea

Collect the request, clarify missing information and prepare a complete handoff. This may help with iterative or modified enquiries. A person checks the quote; no binding price, stock, suitability or order commitment is implied.

Opportunity 03 · Proposed

Engineered solutions and specialist support

Application knowledge. Design. Technical collaboration.

Explore this idea

Organise project context, drawings and questions for a specialist. Qualified people retain responsibility for design, compatibility and safety. Published services do not establish local system access or permission to reuse knowledge.

05.01

A service connected to people and systems

Customer’s assistant → authorised commercial service → Swagelok people and approved systems. Shared product knowledge might be reusable across a network; customer data, prices, inventory and commercial authority must remain appropriately local.

Notes & sources N12 ↗
05.02

A practical starting point

Consider quotation assistance for one approved product category: structured intake, retrieval from a curated knowledge set, clarification of missing information and a complete handoff to a person. Start alongside existing systems, possibly with controlled uploaded data.

Unchanged repeat requests could be confirmable only within approved limits and with authority. Standard changes prepare a quote; unusual needs go to a specialist; discounts or larger commitments go to an account manager. No order is placed by this briefing.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE · LOCAL ONLY

Walk through a quotation request

01 / A request arrives

Fictional customer: “We need the same items as our approved list, with one change.” No real parts, prices or customer records are used.

02 / Clarify the change

Collect the list reference, requested change, quantity and delivery need. Flag technical questions for a qualified specialist; do not infer compatibility.

03 / Prepare the handoff

Organise the request and outstanding questions for a person to review. No quote is issued and no order is placed.

Proposed intake pattern. No live AI, supplier integration or transaction.

Notes & sources N11 ↗
05.03

Measure before claiming an improvement

Potential benefits are better customer experience, greater efficiency, opportunity for growth and a practical starting point. Each is a hypothesis to measure.

Establish a baseline for request completeness, clarification rounds, handling time, customer effort, conversion, specialist capacity and errors. A shorter interaction is not an improvement if it loses important technical information.

Notes & sources N12 ↗
05.04

Questions for our conversation

Where do requests become difficult, and what would this improve rather than duplicate? Which product information and interfaces could be used with permission? Where must technical judgement and commercial approval remain central? What could be reused across distributors, and what must stay local? Is there a narrow, measurable test with an agreed sponsor?

Notes & sources N12 ↗
THE THOUGHT TO TAKE FORWARD

A useful first test would be narrow enough to measure, safe enough to run alongside today’s process, and valuable enough for a customer and the team to choose again.

This is a developing commercial proposition. Products and working technology are at differing stages; intended capabilities must be separated from demonstrated functions.

A founder’s experience

I’ve spent much of my professional life building technology businesses. A year ago, if you’d told me that I could personally develop in a week software that would previously have required a team of six to ten people, six to nine months and hundreds of thousands of pounds, I wouldn’t have believed you.

AI has fundamentally changed the economics of the activity on which I’ve built much of my career. I didn’t predict that change, and I certainly don’t pretend to know exactly how AI will transform other industries.

Our next challenge

Validate with real customers. Develop distribution in an AI-mediated environment. Identify repeatable commercial use cases. Build revenue models and iterate from evidence.

Integration, security, reliable information, operations, accountability and specialist expertise remain valuable and can remain costly. Distribution is not automatically a durable advantage.

Terms and technologies

AIArtificial intelligence

Software that performs tasks associated with human intelligence, such as recognising patterns, generating language or supporting decisions. Capability in one task does not imply dependable judgement in another.

Return to briefing ↑

Generative AI

AI that produces new content, such as text, images, audio or code, from patterns learned during training. A convincing answer can still be wrong.

Return to briefing ↑

Large language modelLLM

An AI model trained on large amounts of language and other data to generate and process text. It can support dialogue, but its output is not an authoritative record of facts.

Return to briefing ↑

Conversational AI

AI designed to interact through dialogue, allowing a person to ask questions, clarify a need and build on earlier exchanges.

Return to briefing ↑

Agentic AI

AI systems arranged to pursue a goal through multiple steps, often using tools. The degree of independence depends on design, permissions and approval rules.

Return to briefing ↑

AI agent

Software that uses an AI model to choose steps and use available tools toward a goal. An agent can have limited authority; being called an agent does not establish autonomy or reliability.

Return to briefing ↑

Personalisation

Adapting responses or services to a person’s preferences or circumstances, using information the system is allowed and able to access.

Return to briefing ↑

Memory and context

Context is information available for a particular interaction. Memory is information a service may retain for later use. What is retained, used or forgotten depends on product settings, limits and permission.

Return to briefing ↑

Plugin

An installed extension that gives an assistant particular instructions, tools or services. Some connect to an external account. Installing one does not authorise every action or guarantee that it will be recommended.

Return to briefing ↑

APIApplication programming interface

A defined way for one software system to request information or an action from another. Access rules, data quality and business authority still need to be established.

Return to briefing ↑

MCPModel Context Protocol

An open standard for connecting AI applications to external information, tools and workflows. It is a connection mechanism, not a permission to transact or proof of autonomous agency.

Return to briefing ↑

A2AAgent2Agent

An open protocol through which separately operated AI agents can exchange messages, tasks, status and results. It can complement MCP. Ordinary software integration does not automatically require it.

Return to briefing ↑

Structured or machine-readable information

Information organised into explicit fields and relationships that software can interpret, such as a business’s name, location and service capabilities. Structure helps interpretation; it does not guarantee accuracy or discovery.

Return to briefing ↑

AI-mediated commerce

Commercial discovery, comparison or interaction in which AI helps connect a customer with a business. Checkout may occur inside an assistant, in an embedded browser or on a separate merchant website.

Return to briefing ↑

Authorisation

Permission for an identified person or system to perform a specific action within defined limits. Access to information is different from authority to accept a price or place an order.

Return to briefing ↑

Human approval

A person explicitly checks and permits a proposed action before it takes effect. The person needs the relevant information and authority; a vague approval prompt is not a substitute for review.

Return to briefing ↑
